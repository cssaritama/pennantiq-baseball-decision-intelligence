# Debutant and Sparse-History Protocol

## Problem

A debuting pitcher has little or no MLB history. A system that produces a precise player-specific plan anyway is fabricating confidence.

## Public protocol

When the active dataset contains fewer than three appearances or roughly 120 pitches:

1. mark the player model as sparse;
2. show the available observations;
3. shrink strongly toward transparent peer priors;
4. use verified handedness and role;
5. request human scouting review;
6. abstain from unsupported player-specific claims.

## Evidence ladder

1. team-owned minor-league pitch tracking;
2. verified arsenal and release data;
3. video and scouting notes;
4. peer groups with comparable pitch shapes and role;
5. opponent performance against those pitch characteristics;
6. early MLB observations, updated sequentially.

## Forbidden shortcuts

- inferring command from velocity alone;
- converting a peer average into a player fact;
- diagnosing readiness or health;
- treating spring or minor-league competition as directly equivalent;
- hiding uncertainty behind an LLM narrative.

## Private Team edition

A team edition can add minor-league tracking, strength/conditioning context, biomechanics, coaching notes and player-development objectives under the organization's governance.
